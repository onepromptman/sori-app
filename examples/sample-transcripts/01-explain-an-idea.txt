DEMO - Explain an idea in your own words

This is an original fictional transcript example. There is no real podcast or speaker behind it.

Welcome to this sample lesson. Our goal is to explain an idea without depending on the wording in our notes. Choose a short passage you have permission to use. Read it once to understand its main point. Then close it and explain the idea aloud as though you were helping a new learner.

For this exercise, imagine a recipe that says to prepare the ingredients before heating the pan. An explanation in your own words might be: get everything ready first so you can concentrate on cooking when the pan is hot. That restates the instruction and gives a reason, without copying the sentence.

Now reopen the passage. Compare your explanation with what the source actually says. Did you leave out a condition? Did you add a reason that the source never supplied? Separate the source's claim from your own interpretation. Write down one correction and try explaining the idea again.

Your practice task is to repeat this sequence with a short lesson of your choice. You are finished when you have an explanation, a comparison with the source, and a correction or a note that no correction was needed. A suggested review activity is to explain the idea again tomorrow before opening your notes. That schedule is a practice suggestion, not a promise of a particular learning result.
