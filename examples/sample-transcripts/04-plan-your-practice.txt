Today we will turn a vague learning intention into a concrete session. "Learn more about presentations" is difficult to finish because it does not name an action or a stopping point. For this exercise, choose one small performance instead, such as explaining a single chart to a listener.

Write down what you will produce. In our fictional example, the output is a short explanation containing the chart's main observation, its unit, and one limitation. Gather the source chart and any notes you need before beginning. Do not invent values if the chart is unclear; identifying missing information is a useful outcome too.

Next, decide how you will check your work. You might compare the explanation with the chart and ask whether every stated value is visible, whether the units are correct, and whether the limitation is clearly separated from the observation. This checklist is a suggested practice aid, not a validated assessment instrument.

Your task is to write a session plan with four headings: Action, Materials, Finished output, and Check. Carry out the action if you have the necessary materials. Afterward, note what you completed and one thing you would adjust next time. A small finished session gives you something specific to review, even when the broader skill still needs more practice.
